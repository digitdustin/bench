Before we ship 5-minute crypto on US

We're approved to list 5-minute Up/Down markets on US. Kalshi has the bigger crypto book and could list the same product within days. This memo covers who actually gains if we go first, why Kalshi hasn't done it, and what we should have in place before we turn it on.

Recommendation
Hold 5m, ship as a package
The short version
  1. The instinct is mostly right. Kalshi's BTC 15-minute book is about 6× ours (~$127M vs ~$26M a day). On its own, a 5-minute series that Kalshi copies would add more dollars to Kalshi than to us. Listing a new contract is a self-certification filing, so their legal lag is about a day. Their real lag is market makers and settlement, which is still measured in weeks, not quarters.
  2. Our crypto growth so far hasn't come at Kalshi's expense. Since BTC 15m launched on US on 9/22, our 7-day share went from 0% to 12.4% against a Q4 goal of 50%, and Kalshi stayed flat at ~$1.16B a week. 87% of Up/Down traders traded sports first. Sports traders who picked up Up/Down grew their sports volume too.
  3. Our bottleneck is reach, and 5m mostly doesn't fix it. Dollars are up 32% week over week, but traders are down 6%. New-to-platform traders fell from 27.5% to 16% of Up/Down traders, and 85% of sports traders still haven't tried it. 5m mostly raises dollars per existing trader. The roadmap's "+50% notional?" estimate for 5m assumes Kalshi doesn't respond.
  4. DeFi showed that 5m spikes and then decays. Volume peaked in March, fell about 70% by August, and only 3.6% of February's players were still betting in August. The spike is the prize, and whoever has the bigger audience on the day both venues list it takes most of the spike.
  5. So we should launch it when it's hard to copy. Ship catch-up features first (more assets, liquidity parity, a faster re-entry loop). Kalshi already has the catch-up features, so shipping them gives Kalshi nothing new. Ship 5m last, bundled with things Kalshi can't match in a week.
Where we stand, 23 days after BTC 15m launched on US

We added share by growing, not by taking it from Kalshi

Who our Up/Down volume comes from:

  • Retail-heavy. ISV app and web are ~55% of crypto taker volume (iOS alone is 50%). PMX, which is likely market-maker and other-FCM flow, is 41%. ISV API is 3.5%. On DeFi 5m, bot-like wallets were ~86% of taker dollars.
  • Not concentrated among ISV accounts. The top 10 accounts are 5.4% of volume, the top 100 are 19.5%, and the top 1,000 are 48.6%. The PMX share has no per-user data, so true concentration is higher.
  • Cross-sold, not cannibalizing. 87% of this week's Up/Down traders traded sports first, and only 5% are crypto-only. Sports traders who picked up Up/Down grew their sports volume 3% and their sports active days 14% week over week. Cross-sold traders are stickier: 47% trade Up/Down on 2+ days, against 38% for crypto-only.
  • Repeat is steady. 33% of first-time Up/Down traders trade again the next day and 51% within 7 days, flat across cohorts. Up/Down D7 for users new to Polymarket is 10.5%, against 9.3% for sports.
  • Shorter is winning. 15m is now 95% of Up/Down volume. 1h fell to $5.1M a week, with 2–4K daily app traders against 19–26K on 15m. This points to real demand for 5m, and also means 5m will mostly pull from 15m. On DeFi, 5m peaked at $385M a week against $126M for 15m.

The DeFi precedent

5-minute was a spike, and what's left is mostly bots

The 5-minute launch in February was real. Bitquery's on-chain read of DeFi's fast crypto markets shows what happened after it.

What the cohort data says

  • 3.6% of February's 5m players were still betting in August.
  • Week-4 retention for 5m wallets was 20.5%, against 48.4% for weekly products.
  • Bot-like wallets were 86% of 5m taker dollars and sat on at least one side of 79% of volume.
  • About 31% of wallets finished a measured week ahead after fees. Retention was highest among the biggest losers (7.2%), which is exactly the cohort regulators will point at.
  • The fee rate peaked at 4.22% in March. Volume kept falling after it was cut to about 1.1%.
Bitquery (Sep 2025–Aug 2026), Pantera/Ally "Crypto on the Clock."

We should be clear about what this means. 5m is very good at creating a launch-week moment. It is weak at building durable retail habit. If Kalshi copies it within two weeks, the moment is shared, and most of it goes to the bigger audience.

Why hasn't Kalshi listed 5m?

Seven plausible reasons, most likely first

I found no on-record Kalshi statement on 5-minute contracts, and no X thread with a stated reason. As of a Sept 4 sweep, Kalshi had 274 crypto series and the shortest was 15 minutes. These reasons are inferred from the evidence cited for each. The chip is my confidence: green is high, amber is medium.

  1. 1

    They don't need it

    Kalshi holds ~85% of US+Kalshi BTC 15m volume today, and by May it had passed Polymarket globally in crypto (60% vs 40% in the week of 5/17). A leader doesn't split its best book to answer a competitor that hasn't threatened it yet.

    High
  2. 2

    It would thin their best fee engine

    BTC 15m earned an estimated ~$60M in fees in September. Short-duration markets made 80% of Kalshi's non-sports fees in the week to Oct 5. Putting the same flow across 3× as many windows thins each book and widens spreads. Market makers, including Kalshi's affiliate Kalshi Trading, would have to quote 288 windows per asset per day.

    High
  3. 3

    Second-mover option

    If we list it first on a regulated US venue, we take the headline and regulatory risk. If nothing happens, they copy it with a one-day self-certification. Press already frames it this way: "if Polymarket's product succeeds without a regulatory clampdown, competitors could launch similar products within months."

    High
  4. 4

    Bad legal timing

    Kalshi lost in the 9th Circuit (Nevada, August) and the 6th Circuit (Ohio/Tennessee, Sept 25), so Supreme Court review looks likelier. The CFTC's public-interest proposal (June 10) drew at least one comment asking for a minimum one-trading-day time to settlement in major asset classes. Kalshi argues event contracts are financial swaps, and that argument is hardest to make for a 300-second BTC coin flip. Critics already compare 5m to the binary options ESMA banned for EU retail in 2018.

    Medium-high
  5. 5

    Settlement design

    Kalshi settles on CF Benchmarks BRTI averaged over the final 60 seconds. On a 5-minute window, that average covers 20% of the window, so a 5m series probably needs a new benchmark spec and license. Polymarket settles on Chainlink Data Streams, which fits short windows better.

    Medium
  6. 6

    Distribution partners set the pace

    Coinbase started routing Kalshi 15m and hourly on Oct 6, through its own FCM, in every state except Nevada. Robinhood is building its own exchange (MIAXdx). A 5-minute binary option inside a broker app is a separate compliance decision for each partner.

    Medium
  7. 7

    They saw the DeFi decay

    The DeFi data is public on-chain: peak in March, ~70% down by August, 3.6% cohort survival, bot-dominated. On that evidence 5m is a headline product, not a retention product.

    Medium

Reasons 1–3 together explain why they would copy fast once we list. Not needing it is a reason to wait, not a reason to refuse. Once a competitor lists 5m on a US exchange and the rival with the bigger book doesn't, that rival risks a "Kalshi doesn't have it" headline. Reasons 4–6 are the friction that could stretch their copy time from days to weeks.

Your core question

If both venues have 5m, who gains more?

In absolute dollars, Kalshi does. If 5m adds the same percentage to each venue's existing crypto book, Kalshi's base is about 6× larger. It also arrives on Coinbase (10 assets) and Robinhood/Webull distribution that we don't have.

In share, it depends on how many traders use both venues, and we don't know that number yet. Our data says the crypto audience is largely our own sports audience. If Kalshi's crypto traders are similarly captive, 5m deepens each venue's own base and share barely moves. Share moves where traders can compare products side by side, so the question becomes what a crypto trader gets from us that they can't get on Kalshi.

our durable gain ≈ (days before Kalshi copies × our 5m incremental volume)
                 + (lasting edge × traders who use both venues)

The first term is small because the copy window is short. Everything in the checklist below is about growing the second term before launch.

Options

Three ways to play it

A. Ship BTC 5m now

Take the moment

  • Fastest headline, and the brand already owns "5-minute"
  • Muddies the 15m read while it's still ramping
  • Hands Kalshi a validated, de-risked product to copy onto a 6× bigger book with broker distribution
RecommendedB. Parity first, 5m as a package

Make the copy expensive

  • Weeks 1–3: ETH/SOL/XRP on 15m/60m/daily, liquidity and fee parity, re-entry loop, protection controls
  • Meanwhile: measure how many traders use both venues, collect D28, line up MM commitments
  • Then: 5m across all listed assets in one launch
C. Don't list 5m

Stay out of the coin-flip fight

  • Avoids regulatory exposure while the circuit split moves toward the Supreme Court
  • Leaves the opening to Kalshi if they decide to go first
  • Gives up the strongest launch moment we have

What I couldn't verify

  • No on-record Kalshi statement on 5m, and no X discussion surfaced through search. The "why" section is inference.
  • PMX flow (41% of crypto) has no per-user data, so concentration and trader counts cover ISV accounts only.
  • Kalshi volume is taker-only and isn't split by channel, so we can't isolate Coinbase-routed flow.
  • Still unmeasured: spread and depth against Kalshi, how many traders use both venues, and pent-up US demand from DeFi geoblocks. Those are the first three things to measure.
  • The Crypto Growth Weekly figures are the team's own read and the page is marked unverified in Notion. My Hex pull is BTC-only, so its share number runs higher than the weekly's all-crypto figure.
